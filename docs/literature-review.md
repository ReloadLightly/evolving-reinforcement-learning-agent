# Literature review: evolutionary experience in delayed-feedback reinforcement learning

**Research date:** 2026-10-11. **Status:** research proposal; no experimental results are reported here.

## Scope and search method

This targeted review asks what a small, controlled experiment can establish about the experience exchanged between evolutionary search and a gradient learner. It is not a systematic review or an exhaustive novelty search.

Searches combined foundational algorithm names with replay, diversity, delayed rewards, robustness, and recent publication years. Representative queries included `Evolution Guided Policy Gradient Reinforcement Learning ERL 2018`, `CEM-RL Combining evolutionary and gradient-based methods for policy search`, `DCRL MAP Elites descriptor conditioned reinforcement learning`, `evolutionary reinforcement learning replay buffer diversity`, `Strategic Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning`, and `evolutionary reinforcement learning non-stationary`. Citation trails, publisher records, author repositories, and author laboratory pages supplied follow-up leads. Recent work was checked through October 2026 where discoverable.

The evidence below comes from original papers, proceedings, author abstracts, and author-maintained resources. Search summaries and third-party explainers were discovery aids, not evidence for technical claims. Full-text passages were inspected for ERL, CEM-RL, DCRL, and SAR-ERL; other entries use the available primary abstract, proceedings record, or author account. Access limitations are identified explicitly.

## Research question selected

**At equal environment-interaction and gradient-update budgets, how does reducing the evolutionary-population share of replay from its pooled-buffer proportion to 0.5 affect TD3 learner sample efficiency, and how does that effect differ between dense rewards and rewards accumulated over 20 steps?**

The proposed tasks are Pendulum-v1 and Reacher-v5, selected for a CPU-only laptop rather than a locomotion benchmark campaign. Each confirmatory run uses 200,000 training transitions. The main comparison keeps collection and evolution unchanged: uniform pooled replay versus source-balanced replay. With an 80:20 population/learner collection allocation, pooled replay should approach a population fraction of 0.8; the actual fraction must be logged, not assumed. An additional population fraction of zero isolates removal of direct population-to-learner experience transfer. Standalone TD3 answers the practical question of whether the hybrid earns its interaction cost.

The hypothesis is that reducing population dominance improves learner sample efficiency, with a larger benefit under delayed feedback, where bootstrapped learning may be more sensitive to heterogeneous experience. This is a testable conjecture: the opposite interaction, task dependence, and inconclusive effects are credible outcomes. The contribution is a **controlled replication and extension of ERL's data-sharing mechanism**, not a new replay algorithm or a claim of state-of-the-art control.

The first bounded stage compares pooled and balanced replay on Pendulum at both delays with ten seeds: 40 runs and eight million training transitions. Those runs can be reused in the full two-task, four-arm, two-delay study: 160 runs and 32 million training transitions, plus evaluation interactions. Runtime remains to be measured in a separate pilot; transition counts are not promises of laptop completion time. The [experiment protocol](protocol.md) specifies execution and evaluation details.

## Foundations and closest evidence

1. **Khadka and Tumer (NeurIPS 2018), [Evolution-Guided Policy Gradient in Reinforcement Learning](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2018/hash/85fc37b18c57097425b52fc7afbb6969-Abstract.html).** ERL couples population-generated replay with periodic injection of a gradient-trained actor. Its hard InvertedDoublePendulum experiment already delays reward to episode end. Therefore, neither evolutionary experience sharing nor studying ERL with delayed rewards is new. Our narrower intervention changes replay provenance while retaining the surrounding algorithm.

2. **Pourchot and Sigaud (ICLR 2019; preprint 2018), [CEM-RL: Combining Evolutionary and Gradient-Based Methods for Policy Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.01222).** CEM-RL refines some sampled policies with gradients and uses evaluated candidates to update its search distribution. The paper includes multi-actor TD3 controls, examines population collapse, and finds that hybrid benefits vary across tasks; Swimmer is a notable exception. This motivates both task replication and a willingness to report negative results.

3. **Fujimoto, van Hoof, and Meger (ICML 2018), [Addressing Function Approximation Error in Actor-Critic Methods](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/fujimoto18a.html).** TD3 addresses actor-critic approximation error with twin critics and delayed policy updates. It is an established, interpretable backbone for the experiment. Replacing ERL's original DDPG with TD3 is a modernization to document, not an exact reproduction or an independent research contribution.

4. **Jaderberg et al. (2017), [Population Based Training of Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.09846).** PBT jointly adapts model parameters and hyperparameter schedules through population exploitation and exploration. It demonstrates a different evolutionary/RL connection. Adding PBT here would confound the experience-sharing question with hyperparameter adaptation, so it belongs in the conceptual background rather than the core baseline set.

5. **Nilsson and Cully (GECCO 2021), [Policy Gradient Assisted MAP-Elites](https://www.cmap.polytechnique.fr/~nikolaus.hansen/proceedings/2021/GECCO/proceedings/proceedings_files/p866-nilsson.pdf).** PGA-MAP-Elites combines genetic variation and critic-guided policy variation to construct diverse, high-performing behavioral archives. It motivates population diagnostics and shows that the intersection extends beyond maximizing a single policy's return. Archive coverage is a distinct objective, so it should not replace learner-return evaluation in this study.

6. **Flageat, Chalumeau, and Cully (2022), [Empirical analysis of PGA-MAP-Elites for Neuroevolution in Uncertain Domains](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13156).** This study analyzes operator contributions and solution reproducibility, including the importance of policy gradients early in search. Mechanistic analysis of evolutionary/gradient cooperation is therefore established research practice. It also warns against treating a single noisy population evaluation as reliable policy quality.

7. **Faldor et al. (GECCO 2023), [MAP-Elites with Descriptor-Conditioned Gradients and Archive Distillation into a Single Policy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03832).** Descriptor-conditioned critics address conflict between reward-improving gradients and behavioral coverage. Archive distillation consolidates diverse policies. This is a strong alternative project direction, but requires a descriptor design and an archive-specific objective beyond the selected replay question.

8. **Faldor et al. (ACM TELO 2024), [Synergizing Quality-Diversity with Descriptor-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.08632).** DCRL-MAP-Elites adds descriptor-conditioned actor injection and studies operator synergies and reproducibility. Its extensive evaluation uses 20 seeds and one million policy evaluations per experiment. Those are policy evaluations, not individual transitions: a faithful full reproduction would substantially expand this project's budget. The authors recommend the [QDax implementation](https://github.com/adaptive-intelligent-robotics/DCRL-MAP-Elites) for new research.

9. **Li et al. (ICML 2024), [EvoRainbow: Combining Improvements in Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning for Policy Search](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/li24cp.html).** EvoRainbow evaluates combinations of interaction modes, policy architectures, evolutionary operators, EA-to-RL effects, and fitness surrogates. It is a contemporary reference for interaction design. Reimplementing its entire combination is unnecessary to identify one replay intervention's effect; omitting it limits any claim of superiority over modern ERL systems.

10. **Templier et al. (CEC 2024), [Genetic Drift Regularization: on preventing Actor Injection from breaking Evolution Strategies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04322).** Actor injection can disrupt an evolution strategy when the actor drifts from its population. The proposed regularization addresses this mismatch. This is negative evidence against assuming all information exchange is beneficial and supports recording injection outcomes while keeping injection behavior identical across replay arms.

11. **Zheng et al. (IEEE TNNLS 2025), [Strategic Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning With Operator Selection and Experience Filter](https://doi.org/10.1109/TNNLS.2025.3596553).** SERL-OS-EF combines operator selection, population-experience filtering, and dynamic mixed sampling. This is particularly close prior art and rules out presenting source-aware ERL replay as a new general idea. The [authors' abstract reproduced in PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40811165/) was accessible; publisher full text was not inspected. Detailed distinctions from its sampling rule remain unresolved.

12. **Cai et al. (IEEE TEVC, online 2025; 2026 issue), [Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning With Late-Start Evolution and Clustering Archive](https://doi.org/10.1109/TEVC.2025.3627631).** LCERL delays evolution, improves mutation, and maintains a behavioral clustering archive to improve population experience. The [authors' laboratory account](https://www.translab.top/index.php/en/2026/01/20/%E3%80%90ep39-%C2%B7-research-highlights%E3%80%91-evolutionary-reinforcement-learning-with-late-start-evolution-and-clustering-archive-published-in-ieee-tevc/) supports these broad mechanisms. Publisher access was gated; the full algorithm and experiments were not independently inspected. This work further weakens broad novelty claims about managing poor population data.

13. **Li et al. (Complex & Intelligent Systems 2026), [SAR-ERL: an evolutionary reinforcement learning optimization method based on state–action co-representation embedding](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40747-026-02306-3).** SAR-ERL augments policy/value learning using state-action representations, dynamics prediction, and reward prediction. It represents a recent representation-learning direction. The selected experiment deliberately fixes representation learning so any observed difference can be attributed more narrowly to replay sampling.

14. **Zhao, Ding, and Dai (Mathematics 2026), [Co-Evolutionary Proximal Distilled Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning with Gated Knowledge Transfer](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/14/6/1078).** Co-PDERL coevolves actor and critic populations with conditional knowledge transfer. This expands the population architecture itself. It is useful evidence of current research breadth, but adding critic evolution would prevent a clean interpretation of the proposed replay intervention.

15. **Li et al. (ICML 2026), [Neuro-evolutionary Continual Reinforcement Learning](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/li26gb.html).** Nevo-CRL develops evolutionary mechanisms for continual learning, including task-specific sparse policy structure and reuse. This establishes recent activity in adaptation and forgetting. A nonstationary-task project would require additional controls for retention, task information, and transfer; those are outside the selected delayed-feedback study.

16. **Lillo and Cheney (2026 preprint; revised June 9), [Beyond Single-Model Optimization: Preserving Plasticity in Continual Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15414).** TeLAPA maintains behaviorally related policy neighborhoods for future reuse and relearning in MiniGrid. Its primary record does not list acceptance. It reinforces that policy archives for adaptation are already active work, rather than an unoccupied alternative research question.

17. **Koohy and Bayne (GECCO 2026, accepted full paper according to authors), [Distributional Value Estimation Without Target Networks for Robust Quality-Diversity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20381).** QDHUAC studies distributional, target-free critics for efficient high-update-to-data QD learning. This motivates logging compute and update counts, but does not establish that source balancing helps TD3. High-UTD critic redesign is another substantial project, not a necessary ingredient here.

18. **Zheng, Cheng, and Tan (2025), [EvoRL: A GPU-accelerated Framework for Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15129).** EvoRL provides accelerated implementations of RL, evolutionary methods, and hybrids. The [authors' repository](https://github.com/EMI-Group/evorl) is a relevant implementation reference. Accelerator-oriented throughput does not establish runtime on the available machine; a local pilot must measure it. An unrelated package also uses the name `evorl`, so provenance matters.

19. **Agarwal et al. (NeurIPS 2021), [Deep Reinforcement Learning at the Edge of the Statistical Precipice](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13264).** This work demonstrates how uncertainty from few independent training runs can change algorithm comparisons. It motivates seed-level intervals, effect estimates, and transparent learning curves. Two tasks do not support broad benchmark claims, and many evaluation episodes cannot substitute for independent training seeds.

20. **Callaghan, Mason, and Mannion (ALA workshop 2023), [Evolutionary Strategy Guided Reinforcement Learning via MultiBuffer Communication](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11535).** ES-TD3Buffers separates replay to limit domination by poor evolutionary trajectories. The authors' abstract directly supports the proposed study's motivation while establishing close prior art. Multiple buffers and controlling population influence must not be presented as new contributions.

21. **Patterson et al. (JMLR 2024), [Empirical Design in Reinforcement Learning](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v25/23-0183.html).** This methodological paper addresses statistical assumptions, implementation sensitivity, hyperparameter choices, and experimenter bias. It supports separating pilot decisions from confirmatory runs and documenting baseline construction. It does not certify the adequacy of this project's seed count or specific budget.

## What this review does and does not justify

The literature supports a tension: population experience can broaden exploration, while its quality and compatibility with the learner can matter. It does not establish the sign or size of the proposed replay-by-delay interaction. SERL-OS-EF and LCERL are especially important overlap checks; the original ERL delay experiment prevents claiming that delayed feedback itself is a new test.

The selected design favors interpretability over algorithm breadth. Alternatives considered were adaptive replay filtering, QD archive construction, continual adaptation, PBT, and high-UTD critic redesign. Each introduces additional mechanisms or objectives already studied in close recent work. Fixed replay proportions provide a manageable first experiment with informative null results.

Several boundaries must remain explicit. Batching rewards and flushing the final remainder preserves undiscounted episode return, but generally changes the discounted objective and introduces hidden reward-history dependence unless modeled. It is therefore a delayed-feedback stress test, not automatically a pure credit-assignment intervention. Replay balancing can also change transition age and reuse; diagnostics should measure these rather than claim they are unchanged. The zero-population-replay arm retains population costs and actor injection, so it is not standalone TD3. All comparisons must count population rollout interactions and match learner updates.

Search coverage and gated full texts prevent a first-of-its-kind claim. Pendulum and Reacher are small control problems: success would not establish performance on locomotion, high-dimensional policies, or QD archives. A 20-step reward batch also occupies different fractions of their episode horizons, so task differences cannot be attributed to replay alone. Conclusions should concern the tested implementations, two tasks, two feedback schedules, and specified budgets. The README should become a completed scientific report only after those experiments produce inspectable data.
